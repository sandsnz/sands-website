Memory making

When a baby dies, the time you have with them may feel impossibly short. Some parents choose to take photographs, create keepsakes or find other ways to remember their baby. Others do not. There is no right way to do this, and nothing here is something you must complete.

What feels right may change from one moment to the next. You can say yes, say no or change your mind. These are simply ideas shared by other bereaved parents.

In hospital or soon after birth

Depending on your circumstances, some of these options may not be possible or may not feel right. You might choose:

  • Taking photographs of your baby, including their hands, feet or profile, if you would like to. Some hospitals work with volunteer photographers such as Heartfelt, a volunteer organisation of professional photographers who provide free, gentle photographic memories to families experiencing baby loss.
  • Asking about hand and foot prints or casts, where these are available
  • Keeping a blanket, hat, name band, scan photograph or another item that feels meaningful to you
  • Keeping a lock of hair, cot card or other small keepsakes in a memory box
  • Writing a letter to your baby or asking whānau to write a message

If there is something you would like to keep, you can ask staff to make sure it is set aside for you.

Support packs often include materials that help with this. Support resources · Certificate of Life

Later, when you're ready

There is no time limit on remembering your baby. If you did not or could not create memories at the time, you have not missed your chance.

Below are some ideas for creating further memories to treasure.

  • Jewellery, candles, gardens or other personal remembrances
  • Writing your baby's story, gathering photographs or inviting whānau to write messages
  • Accessing these free or donated services offered to bereaved families in New Zealand — see Free memorial services

You don't have to do this alone

Hospital staff, your midwife, funeral directors and Sands volunteers can talk through what may be possible and support the choices that feel right for you. Find a local group or contact us.
